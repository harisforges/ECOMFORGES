# Hyperlocal Angles — KL/Selangor

The copywriting rules call for natural hyperlocal context. This file exists so
that "natural" has a definition, because the failure mode is name-dropping:
swapping Puchong for Klang in an otherwise generic sentence adds nothing and
reads as filler.

## The test

A location earns its place only when the sentence would be **less true** if you
swapped the location out. If any town could be substituted without changing the
meaning, delete the location rather than picking a different one.

Weak — the town is decoration:

> Kedai di Puchong pun boleh scale dengan strategi yang betul.

Stronger — the town carries a real difference:

> Kedai di Shah Alam tak semestinya perlukan strategi yang sama dengan kedai
> di Bangi.

## Approved locations

Puchong, Shah Alam, Petaling Jaya, Subang, Klang, Kajang, Bangi, Cyberjaya,
Setia Alam.

Use the form sellers actually use in conversation — PJ is normal in English
copy, Petaling Jaya reads formal. Do not stack more than two locations in one
piece; a list of towns is the same filler problem in longer form.

## Angles that hold without proof

Each of these is a structural observation about how selling differs across the
Klang Valley. None of them asserts a measured result, so none of them needs
proof to be usable today.

**Catchment and delivery.** A seller shipping mostly within the Klang Valley has
a different delivery-cost and return profile from one shipping nationwide. Same
product, different margin per order, so the same discount does different damage.

**Pickup and self-collect.** Self-collect is realistic for a Setia Alam or Subang
seller with local repeat buyers, and largely irrelevant for one whose orders are
spread across the peninsula. It changes what a "free shipping" threshold is for.

**Warehouse and home operations.** Many Klang Valley SMEs run from a home or a
small shoplot rather than a fulfilment centre. Order volume that looks like
success on a dashboard can be the thing breaking packing time and response rate.

**Physical shop plus online.** A seller with a Klang or Kajang storefront has
walk-in demand that never appears in platform analytics. Online numbers read
worse than the business actually is, and decisions made on those numbers alone
will be wrong.

**Weekday and weekend split.** Cyberjaya and PJ office catchments behave
differently across the week from residential Bangi or Setia Alam. Campaign
timing copied from a generic calendar ignores this.

**Language mix.** The same product can need different copy in Subang and in
Kajang. A store running one language for everything is making a choice, usually
without realising it is a choice.

## What never to imply

Never suggest EcomForges has local market share, a client cluster in a
particular town, or knowledge of specific competitors in an area unless that is
true and evidenced. "We work with a lot of sellers in Puchong" is a proof claim
and falls under the same rule as any other.
