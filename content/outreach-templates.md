# Outreach templates — cold email and WhatsApp

For Malaysian SME sellers on Shopee, Lazada, TikTok Shop and own web stores.

Voice follows the house rules: British spelling, short sentences, no hedging, no
emoji, no exclamation marks. The consultancy advises; the client executes.

---

## The hook, and why it is this one

Every competitor opens with "we grow your sales". The one thing we can say that
nobody else says, and that the reader can verify in their own dashboard in two
minutes, is the **cross-platform conversion gap**:

> The same catalogue, at the same prices, in the same month, converts at
> materially different rates on Shopee and Lazada — and most sellers have never
> compared the two properly, because the platforms calculate conversion
> differently and the dashboards are never opened side by side.

That is specific, surprising, and testable. It also happens to be the thing our
tooling is actually built to find. Lead with it whenever the prospect sells on
two or more channels.

For single-channel sellers the hook changes — see the variant in Email 1b.

# EMAIL

## Rules for the send

| | |
|---|---|
| Sending domain | A **separate** domain, never `@ecomforges.com` — cold volume damages the domain your client work goes out on |
| Warm-up | Two to three weeks before real volume |
| Daily cap | 30–50 per inbox, per day |
| First email | **No links, no attachments, no images.** All three hurt deliverability and read as a blast |
| Signature | Plain text. A name and the company. No banner |
| Opt-out | One line, every email. Required under PDPA and it protects the domain |
| Follow-ups | Same thread. Two, then stop |

Personalisation tokens: `{{first_name}}`, `{{brand}}`, `{{category}}`,
`{{platform_a}}`, `{{platform_b}}`.

Do not send a single one of these without opening the store first. Thirty
seconds on their listings makes the difference between a reply and a report.

## Subject lines

Lower case, short, no pitch. Test in pairs.

- `{{brand}} on {{platform_b}}`
- your {{platform_a}} vs {{platform_b}} numbers
- a question about {{brand}}
- {{category}} conversion on {{platform_b}}

Avoid anything with "free", "boost", "skyrocket", "10x", or a currency figure in
the subject. Those are spam-filter bait and they read like every other blast.

## Email 1 — multi-channel seller

```
Subject: your {{platform_a}} vs {{platform_b}} numbers

Hi {{first_name}},

You sell {{category}} on both {{platform_a}} and {{platform_b}}. Same products,
same prices, same month.

In most Malaysian stores we look at, one of those channels converts at close to
half the rate of the other, and the seller has never compared them side by side
because the two dashboards calculate conversion differently.

Where that gap exists, it is usually the cheapest revenue in the business. No
extra traffic, no extra ad spend, same catalogue.

Worth checking in your account? Send me 30 days from both dashboards and I will
tell you which way the gap runs and roughly what it is worth. No charge, and no
obligation to work with us afterwards.

{{sender_name}}
EcomForges

Reply STOP and I will not contact you again.
```

## Email 1b — single-channel seller

```
Subject: {{brand}} on {{platform_a}}

Hi {{first_name}},

You are selling {{category}} on {{platform_a}} and, from the outside, doing it
properly — the listings are tidy and the reviews are there.

The question I would ask in your position is which single number is holding the
rest back this month. Traffic, conversion, basket size and campaign dependency
all look like problems at once, so most sellers work on all four and move none
of them.

We read 30 days of your dashboard and name the one that is actually costing you
money, with the arithmetic shown.

Want me to run it? No charge, and no obligation afterwards.

{{sender_name}}
EcomForges

Reply STOP and I will not contact you again.
```

## Email 2 — follow-up, three working days later, same thread

```
Hi {{first_name}},

One thing I should have said in the first email: we do not touch your account.

We are advisers, not an agency. You keep your logins, your budget and your team.
We read the data, name the constraint, and give your team three moves to run over
30 days. Then we check whether the number moved.

That matters because an agency that logs in and changes things leaves you
dependent on it. A team that knows why a change worked can repeat it without us.

Still happy to read your 30 days if you want a second opinion.

{{sender_name}}
```

## Email 3 — close the loop, one week after Email 2

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I will leave it here so I am not cluttering your inbox.

If it is useful later: the fastest thing you can do yourself is open your
conversion rate on each channel for the same 30 days and compare them. If one is
far behind the other on the same catalogue, the gap is the listings or the
pricing on that channel, not the market.

Good luck with {{brand}}.

{{sender_name}}
```

# WHATSAPP

## Read this before sending anything

WhatsApp is not an email list. Meta requires **opt-in before a business messages
a person**, and business-initiated messages must use a template approved in
advance. Blasting a cold list gets the number banned, usually within days.

If that number is also the one your existing clients message you on, a ban takes
those conversations with it.

So none of the templates below are cold blasts. Each one is for a case where
there is already a basis to message:

| Template | When it is allowed |
|---|---|
| A | They enquired, downloaded something, or filled a form |
| B | A mutual client introduced them |
| C | They agreed on a call or by email that you would send it |
| D | A past lead who went quiet |
| E | Logistics for a booked session |

Templates A, B and D need submitting for approval in WhatsApp Manager before
first use if you are on the Business API. On a normal WhatsApp Business app with
a person-by-person send, approval does not apply — but the ban risk from volume
still does.

Keep it under 300 characters where you can. Malaysian business WhatsApp is
direct; a wall of text reads as a template.

## A — inbound enquiry, same day

```
Hi {{first_name}}, {{sender_name}} from EcomForges. Thanks for getting in touch
about {{brand}}.

Quickest way to make this useful: send me 30 days from your Shopee and Lazada
dashboards, screenshots are fine. I will come back with which channel is
underperforming and what it is worth.

No charge for that part.
```

## B — referral introduction

```
Hi {{first_name}}, {{sender_name}} here. {{referrer_name}} suggested I message
you about {{brand}}.

We work with Malaysian sellers on Shopee, Lazada and TikTok. We do not run your
ads or touch your account — we read the data, name the one thing costing you
money this month, and your team runs it.

Open to me taking a look at 30 days?
```

## C — after they agreed to it

```
Hi {{first_name}}, as promised.

Send whenever you have them: 30 days of Shopee and Lazada, screenshots of the
analytics pages are enough. Revenue, visitors, orders, conversion and ad spend.

I will read them and come back within two working days.
```

## D — re-engagement, at least 30 days quiet

```
Hi {{first_name}}, {{sender_name}} from EcomForges. We spoke about {{brand}}
back in {{month}}.

No pitch. Campaign season is coming and the sellers who do well in it are the
ones who fixed their conversion before the traffic arrived, not during.

If you want that read on your numbers, the offer stands. If not, I will leave
you to it.
```

## E — session confirmed

```
Hi {{first_name}}, confirmed for {{day}} at {{time}}.

Two things beforehand so we use the hour properly:

1. 30 days of data from every channel you sell on
2. One person from your side who owns execution between sessions

We will name one constraint and three moves. Your team runs them, and we measure
whether the number moved next cycle.
```

# What to say, and what not to

## Say

- **One constraint per cycle.** A list of ten problems is why nothing gets fixed.
- **You keep control.** We do not log into the account or spend the budget.
- **From RM499 a month, month to month.** No lock-in, no notice period.
- **We turn people down.** If the problem is margin or operations, growth work is
  not worth paying for yet, and we say so.
- **The number moves or we say why.** Specifically: either the sprint was not
  executed, or our reading was wrong.

## Do not say

- Any guaranteed result, percentage, or timeframe. There is no guarantee, and a
  loose promise in writing is one a client will hold you to.
- "We will grow your sales." Everyone says it and it commits you to the outcome
  rather than the method.
- **The "900+ brands" figure — unless it is literally true and you can show it.**
  A social proof claim that cannot be evidenced is the fastest way to lose a
  sophisticated prospect, and under Malaysian consumer protection law a
  misleading representation in trade is an offence.
- Anything in the subject line with a currency figure or "free".

## PDPA, briefly

The Personal Data Protection Act governs commercial electronic messages in
Malaysia. B2B outreach to a business address published for that purpose is the
defensible end of the spectrum; a bought list of personal numbers is the other.

Whatever the source, three things are not optional: an opt-out in every email,
honouring it immediately, and keeping a record of where each contact came from.

Take actual legal advice before running a large campaign. This file is written by
a marketing function, not a lawyer.
